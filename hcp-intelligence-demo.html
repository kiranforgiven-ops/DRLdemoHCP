Method · constrained allocation

Doctor estimates, with uncertainty.

Net retail demand is allocated within each brick. Scores show relative priority; estimated potential is a separate quantity. Use the controls to see the allocation and intervals respond.

Transparent allocation assumptions

Illustrative starting weights; sums are normalized within each brick.

Netted demand = brick category sales × addressable retail share. Doctor weights reflect category relevance, practice capacity and public/activity signals. Synthetic training-audit records adjust specialty-level weight multipliers; held-out audit records are excluded. This lightweight example does not model actual prescription behavior.

Brick totals reconcile

Every brick’s estimates sum to its net addressable total in each resample.

The 10th and 90th percentiles across 1,000 seeded perturbations form the displayed 80% interval. Wider intervals reflect greater input uncertainty. This is a prototype resampling procedure; calibration must be measured on a separate audit sample before operational use.

How to read this back-test

1
Seal a subset
Hold-out audit truth is not used in that doctor’s estimate.
2
Compare point estimates
Rank correlation tests ordering; top-decile capture measures how many actual top-potential doctors were found.
3
Check interval coverage
Coverage is the share of held-out truths inside the nominal 80% interval. Small samples make this noisy.
A useful pilot needs a sample size and acceptance thresholds agreed in advance, plus a method appropriate to the data. A result from this synthetic roster must not be presented as efficacy proof.
